# MQSF 2026 presentation

Status: the 19-slide keynote and final LiH/device evidence are implemented and
locally verified. Publishing the updated preview and physical clicker/projector
rehearsal remain.

## Goal and scope

Deliver **System Software for Quantum Computing: From the Metal to the User** as
a 35-minute keynote for a 1920 × 1080 projector and about 400 people. MQSC is
the primary affiliation, with TUM and the open-source ecosystem credited.
Forward/back clicker keys control every slide, build, and replay.

The deliverable is self-contained HTML and an offline ZIP. Native tools capture
compiler output, execution, and application results before packaging. The
browser renders those artifacts and replays recorded events without a backend or
network connection.

## Current design and ownership

Sources live in `presentations/mqsf2026/`. `presentation.js` defines 19 slides,
progressive builds, and timed playbacks. `presentation.css` owns the 16:9
layout; `visuals.js`, `circuit.js`, and `motion.js` provide vector scenes,
circuit views, and transitions. `presenter-notes.md` owns the rehearsal story;
the presentation `README.md` owns controls, capture commands, evidence limits,
and validation entry points.

The narrative opens with the integration problem and shared stack, completes a
LiH quantum-classical workflow, follows one of its measurement circuits through
device-aware compilation, then explains structured programs and adaptive QIR
execution. It closes with MQT Core and the ecosystem. Timed builds distinguish
imaginary-time evolution from measured wall-clock execution. Forward completes a
one-shot replay before advancing; it stops and advances a looping replay.

`capture_programs.py` records parity-feedback, iterative QPE, and
repeat-until-success compiler artifacts. `capture_execution.py` records actual
DDSIM C-interface calls and merges the application. `capture_afqmc.py` records
the LiH application. `capture_devices.py` compiles that application's first
shadow circuit against three dated device models and executes the unchanged
payloads on DDSIM. The presentation README documents merging the target and
reference metadata into `captures/demo.json.gz`. That curated fixture and
`captures/references.json` are retained; raw capture intermediates are ignored.

## Durable evidence decisions

LiH/STO-3G at 1.6 Å uses a frozen Li 1s core and CAS(2 electrons, 3 sigma
spatial orbitals): six spin orbitals and 15 determinants. The fixed trial was
tuned classically to nearly the exact active-space state. Measured matchgate
shadows supply the trial overlaps; a native 2,048-program QDMI job collects 256
shots per program. Four local CPU workers propagate 128 walkers for 240 steps
for each of the shadow and Hartree–Fock trials.

Dense 15-state overlap, local-energy, and force-bias contractions are an exact
small-space adaptation. They replace a pinned upstream estimator that fails an
independent larger-space energy check, while retaining its phaseless update. The
plotted energy is a weighted mixed estimate; its band conditions on the single
shadow data set and excludes shadow and systematic errors. The near-FCI trial
explains the initial estimate. This is a verified workflow demonstration, not
quantum VQE, a scalability result, or quantum advantage.

Device evidence uses AWS Braket metadata for Emerald and IonQ Forte-1 and the
public IBM `ibm_miami` Nighthawk r1 snapshot calibrated in April 2026. The
compiler queries the actual local QDMI SC model populated from those snapshots,
not the vendor's live provider. IonQ synthesis stops at its supported QIS
interface; physical GPI/GPI2/ZZ synthesis remains provider-owned. All captured
payload execution is ideal DDSIM. Device geometry and smooth transitions are
schematic; refinement endpoints, gate operands, and timing records are real.

The separate teaching programs preserve counted loops, reset, and feedback.
Bounded unrolling retains measurement-dependent corrections. Iterative QPE with
four logical qubits, eight output bits, and phase 1/3 supplies the timed runtime
histogram. Opt-in `MQT_MQSF_SHOT_TRACE` uses an isolated single-program worker,
serial per-shot execution, continuous RNG, and retained terminal-state support.
Its Linux monotonic timestamps include instrumentation overhead and do not
measure normal batch throughput.

The light MQSC design uses original SVG scenes, official assets with provenance,
embedded fonts, and tightly cropped path-only equations. Source links and QR
codes are optional resources. Read the Docs packages recorded evidence only; its
presentation-branch override stays separate from ordinary Core docs.

## Validation

The final LiH raw capture and merged application agree exactly. Independent
checks rebuild its Hamiltonian from chemist integrals and explicit fermion
operators, verify the Hubbard–Stratonovich identity, compare complex Pfaffian
and cached overlaps, and check local-energy and force-bias contractions. Saved
energies, standard errors, and effective populations recompute from walker
observations. All indexed native programs, ordered shots, histograms, payload
hashes, task intervals, process identities, and adapter call bounds agree.

The local suite passes 46 tests and five subtests. Offline Chromium checks all
19 slides, 60 builds, six finite replays, three loops, 5,237 circuit windows,
215 feedback dependencies, interrupted morphs, exact result progress, and a
19-page print export. FullHD geometry and vector assets pass with no external
requests or browser errors. Repository lint passes. A visual review inspected
all slides; physical rehearsal and hosted results are separate checks. Reproduce
validation with the commands in the presentation README rather than relying on
